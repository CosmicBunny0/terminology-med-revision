p9 Medical Terminology definition: "It is the language used to precisely describe the human body including its components, processes, conditions affecting it, and procedures performed upon it." "Medical terminology is used in the field of medicine and pharmacy."
p10 Most medical terms are a combination of word parts: prefix, Root/Stem, Suffix (pencil). Most of them comes from Latin or Greek words. Other language such as English, German and French.
p11 Prefix: a word part attached to the beginning of a word root to modify its meaning. Suffix: a word part attached to the end of the word root to modify its meaning. Root: the word part that is the core of the word.
p12 Combining Vowel: a word part, usually an "O" or "i".
p13 Carcinogen = carcin/o/gen: Carcin (Root)=Cancer + O (Combining Vowel) + gen (Suffix)=Producing → "Substance that promotes the formation of cancer".
p14 Pericarditis: Peri prefix=Around + card Root=Heart + itis Suffix=Inflammation → "inflammation around the heart".
p15 Root + suffix: Lipemia = lip (Root)=Fat + emia (Suffix)=Blood condition → "A condition of fat in blood".

p19 Prefix + Root + suffix: hyperlipemia = Hyper prefix=Excessive + lip Root=Fat + emia Suffix=Blood condition → "A condition of excessive fat in blood".
p20 Cardiac = Cardi/ac: Cardi root=Heart + ac suffix=Pertaining to / Related to → "Pertaining to heart". p21 thank you.
## L2 (Dr Shaimaa) "Medical Term Components"
p1 AI title (exclude). p2 fish. p3 hyperlipemia repeat. p4 Combining vowels #1 (between root and suffix). p5 #2 Root + Root.
p6 Combining vowels: "When a medical term has more than one root, each is joined by a vowel"; [o] most common; [i] second most common; used to join: i- root to root, ii- root to suffix.
p7 Additional Roots {often a medical term is formed of two or more roots}: Hyperlipoproteinemia = Hyper/lip/o/protein/emia (prefix, Root, vowel, Root, Suffix) = "A condition of excessive fat and protein in blood".
p8 Why we use Combining vowels: To ease pronunciation. Is their presence mandatory? Not all medical terms have combining vowels.
p9 Rules for Forming and Spelling Medical Terms: 1. Memorizing and spelling basic medical word components are the first steps for learning how to form medical terms. 2. The next step is to construct the words using the following steps.
p10 Rule 1: A combining vowel is used to join root to root as well as to any suffix beginning with a consonant (slide: "combing", "constant"). Electr (Root=Electric)+cardi (Root=Heart)+gram (Suffix=Record) = Electr/o/cardi/o/gram = "Electrical record of the heart".
p11 image "-GRAPHY; -GRAM; -GRAPH": Electrocardiogram (record), Electrocardiograph (machine), Electrocardiography (process) [3rd-party picture; labels only].
p12-13 Rule 2: A combining vowel is not used before a suffix that begins with a vowel. Vas (Root=Vessel)+Ectomy (Suffix=excision استئصال) = Vasectomy = "Excision of a vessel".
p14 Rule 3: If the root ends in a vowel and the suffix begins with the same vowel, drop the final vowel from the root and do not use a combining vowel. cardi + itis → Carditis = "Inflammation of heart".
p15 Rule 4: when a prefix ends in a vowel and the root begins with a vowel, the final vowel is dropped from the prefix: para (prefix=Alongside of)+enter (Root=Intestine)+al (Suffix=Pertaining to) = Parenteral = "Pertaining to alongside of the intestine".
p16 Rule "4" (slide numbers it 4 again → it's rule 5): Most often a combining vowel is inserted between two roots even when the second root begins with a vowel: Cardi (Root=Heart)+esophag (Root)+eal (Suffix=Pertaining to) = cardi/o/esophag/eal = "pertaining to heart and esophagus".
p17 Formation of medical terms: All medical terms build from the root. Prefixes and suffixes are attached to the root to modify its meaning. Often two or more roots are linked before being modified.
p17-19 Silent letters at the start of a word and unusual pronunciation (Letter|Pronunciation|Example): Ch=K Chemical; C (before a,o,u)=K Cavity, Colon, Cure; C (before e,i)=s Cephalic, Cirrhosis; Dys=dis Dystrophy; Eu=u euphoria; Gn=n gnathic; Ph=f pharmacy; Pn=n pneumonia; Ps=s pseudo; Pt=t ptosis; Rh=r rheumatic; X=z xenon. p2 Color Code for Medical Terminology (highlighters): Prefix- = blue: Meaning before the root; Modifies the meaning; e.g. hyper-, hypo-, anti-, pre-. -Root- = green: Main meaning of the term; Usually indicates the organ, system or action; e.g. cardi-, nephro-, hepat-, gastr-. -Suffix = pink: Meaning after the root; Indicates condition, procedure, disease, drug class, etc.; e.g. -itis, -emia, -ology, -logy, -ase.
p3 Prefix- categories: Time and position; Disease; Degree; Negative prefix.
p4 PREFIX - TIME AND/OR POSITION (examples colour-coded: blue prefix/green root/pink suffix):

Post- | After, behind | Postoperative (Post|operat|ive) | After a surgical operation

p9 SUFFIX intro: Suffixes are the ending part of medical terms. Suffixes tend to bring an action to the medical terms. Medical suffixes can be categorized into: Specialties; Condition of; Disease; Small; Surgery; Adjective suffix. ("-Suffix and their pronunciation")
